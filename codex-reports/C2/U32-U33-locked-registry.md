# U32/U33: preserve the lock and create an explicit corrected revision

**Decision requested.** Should a damaged RCN/EEIC registry in a locked cycle be correctable, and can the cycle still be called locked? Recommend a separate, traceable corrected revision; never silently edit the original locked snapshot.

**What is established.** The pinned ledger describes duplicate `K1` and reserved `__none__` cases with expected USAF `$900` and ANG `$200`, versus `$1,800` and `$400` in the defective export. B2 is assigned to retest the draft separately; C2 claims no result from that batch. The supplied draft policy refuses duplicate, reserved and non-text registry keys in handover files, while a person’s own board imported whole preserves its lists and reports them. Base source inspection shows that `cycLock` copies the RCN/EEIC registry with the cycle, and `cycView` reads the kept registry. Changing those entries changes the interpretation of historical pointers, even if the requirement’s dollar values remain untouched.

**Options and consequences.**

1. **Keep the original cycle immutable; refuse unsupported handovers or ambiguous exports.** The historical evidence and the person’s full board remain available. A corrected file must come from its owner, or the owner creates a new valid cycle. This is the smallest immediate policy, but someone may be unable to export a usable history until a correction path exists. A preserved whole-board import must not advertise a malformed cycle as cleared for every export.
2. **Allow in-place registry edits inside the lock.** A title correction can look harmless, but duplicate keys may disagree about codes and a reserved-key rename needs all references handled. Editing the locked list can change classification or office/service grouping after the fact. Calling the modified object the same original lock weakens the guarantee that it is the record as kept at that time.
3. **Create a corrected revision with an explicit link to the original.** Keep the original years, registry, metadata and pointer values recoverable. Work on a copy, record exactly which registry entries or pointers changed and why, and give the corrected revision a new identity, author/time and prominent “Corrected revision of …” label. Reports from that revision identify it. The original remains accessible as the historical source, including its limitations.

**Recommendation and boundaries.** Choose option 3 as a later, separately approved correction feature; retain option 1 until it is implemented and proved. Do not add an ordinary registry editor to a locked view. A whole-board recovery import is a preservation route, not a bypass that certifies the registry. Refusal must explain which list/key prevents a handover or export and leave the incoming file and current work untouched.

**Correction evidence.** The person must resolve an ambiguous duplicate; the software must not pick its first or last code automatically. A reserved key can be renamed only with an explicit, complete reference map. Before committing a revision, show original versus corrected registry and pointers, and compare by service, appropriation, effective FY, requirement and CLIN. Dollar totals must remain USAF `$900` and ANG `$200` for the two literal cases; changed classifications must be called out rather than hidden by matching totals. Read actual XLSX after correction and after saved-file reopening; prove the original bytes and working plan are unchanged, cancellation writes nothing, failures preserve both copies, and ordinary-key controls still work. Counting a bag once prevents doubling but does not alone prove its RCN/EEIC attribution is right.

**Cost of the choice.** A new revision adds metadata, storage/recovery, revision selection and cross-export labeling work. The lock’s useful guarantee survives: the original is unchanged; the corrected interpretation is visibly a different record. In-place editing is cheaper but cannot honestly promise both correction and an untouched historical lock.
